How we work · the stable layer

Two layers: one stays, one flows

The central question is how deep expertise survives inside a Domain. The answer is sub-domains — the tech home of the experts, the on-call rota and the inner-source repos. People rotate through pods; their sub-domain, manager and rota stay put.

Sub-domain — stable, always on Pod — spawns for a problem, then dissolves
The one lineEvolution, not revolution. Nobody's on-call rota, or expertise home changes on day one. We map today's teams to sub-domains as-is, then let pods form on top as problems get picked up.
MOVE 1

Name the sub-domains

Turn today's on-call teams into stable sub-domains that own services, expertise and rotas — the tech home experts return to.

MOVE 2

Flow pods on top

Charter pods around the business problems, staffed from sub-domains, dissolved when the problem is solved.

MOVE 3

EMs stay with people

Managers are allocated to people, not pods, so stability and growth hold steady while work reshuffles.

MOVE 4

Make readiness visible

A Proficiency Matrix shows who can safely be pulled where — and gates any future on-call consolidation.

OE & on-call — owned by the sub-domain, not the pod

Where OE lives

Operational excellence, on-call and inner-source are owned by the sub-domains via SMEs, not the pods — continuous, so nothing evaporates when a pod dissolves. Bigger OE work still gets pod capacity via an "Operational Scalability & Excellence" charter when needed.

~20% at sub-domain level

Propose ~20% of engineer time held at the sub-domain — syncs, runbooks, the sub-domain backlog, staying current. This also absorbs the gaps while a problem is still being framed.

Ways of working — guardrails, not workflow

Day-to-day is the pod's call

We don't prescribe spec flow, tools or process — the pod is AI-native by default. We set guardrails (charter DoR/DoD, dependency modes), not the workflow.

How we work · people & roles

Who owns what — and how oversight holds as people rotate

Stability for people, flexibility for work. The whole point of anchoring on-call and people in sub-domains is so work can reshuffle without destabilising anyone. This tab pins the role expectations agreed on 26 Aug and answers two open questions: how Staff supports pods, and how EMs keep oversight when reports move pod to pod.

Role expectations

Pod Lead

A mantle, not a title or layer — worn by a senior/staff IC (or an EM) leading one pod. Owns one customer problem end to end, discovery to measured result. Reports to the Director for delivery; people management stays with the EM.

  • Technical direction & architecture — sets the bar, drives design/ADRs, orchestrates the pod's AI agents (human in the loop).
  • Delivery ownership — the big shift off the EM: planning, sequencing, execution, risk & dependencies, release and predictability.
  • Built-in quality bar — owns the pod's quality; QE coaches on strategy and evals.
  • Decides how the problem gets solved; leads the monthly readouts. Success = customer impact, not output.

Full responsibilities & delivery breakdown → Confluence ↗

SME — one per sub-domain

An earned mantle carried on top of the role — recognition, not an assignment. Keeps on-call and inner-source (capabilities) healthy.

  • Runs regular syncs so members stay current on what pods changed in the sub-domain's services.
  • Keeps on-call runbooks alive and accurate.
  • Owns a sub-domain backlog with the sub-domain EM — OE, tech debt, resilience, upgrades.
  • Performs disaster roleplays; raises risks & concerns to the sub-domain EM.
  • Supports the EM on on-call rota health — training, incident trends.

Engineering Manager

Allocated to people, not pods — owns the engineering system the pods run within (people, skills, capacity, on-call, platform health), without adding a layer below the Domain. The job shifts from owning delivery to growing talent and engineering excellence.

  • People & performance — line management, growth, calibration and a sustainable pace, with evidence gathered across changing pod assignments.
  • Skills, succession & SME coverage — the Proficiency Matrix as source of truth for staffing, gaps and backup SME cover on critical flows.
  • Capacity & planning — makes capacity, hidden work (KTLO, incidents, ops load) and trade-offs visible; advises on pod formation, sequencing and rebalancing.
  • Engineering & platform health — reliability, modernisation and stewardship, balanced and tracked by health metrics (SLOs, incident recurrence).
  • Inner-source (GBP) enablement & on-call — unblocks cross-pod / cross-brand flow and PR review lead time; keeps on-call fair, sustainable and improving.
  • AI-native adoption — across build, test, review and ops, with evals and guardrails.

Full accountabilities & success metrics → Engineering Lead JD (draft).

Quality Engineer (QE)

A shared, cross-pod coaching capability — not a gate. Never a required approver to ship; quality is built in, not tested in at the end. Careers & priorities sit with the EMs / Directors for now.

  • Coach, don't do — teaches pods to own their quality from ideation, building with the pod rather than for it.
  • Owns the quality system — standards, testability reviews, tooling, E2E automation (co-designed) and dashboards; pods own execution (their tests and test plans).
  • Depth over breadth; time is prioritised top-down — coaching non-negotiable, automation protected, low-priority work cut first.

How Staff supports pods

Keep two support layers distinct so they don't blur. The SME works inside one sub-domain (above). Staff / Principal (Dias) works across the Domain, above the pods — deliberately un-booked to any single pod so they stay available.

Shapes charters at Gate 0

Sizes each problem with the PM Lead and Director — how much work, which expertise — so a charter is realistic before a pod spins.

Reviewer across pods

The design / architecture reviewer that keeps decisions coherent between pods, and owns the cross-cutting concerns no single pod owns — platform, resilience, the InnerSource capabilities themselves.

Embeds when it's hard

Drops into a pod for a genuinely hard problem — the "Embed owner" arm of the dependency model (Operating model) — with a named exit condition.

Grows the SMEs

Mentors SMEs and Pod Leads, and is building the business-oriented metrics (Metrics). Net: a force multiplier and consistency guardrail, not a pod member.

EM oversight when reports rotate pod → pod

The anchorLine management follows the sub-domain, not the pod. A report's home sub-domain fixes their EM, whichever pod they're seconded to — so oversight never actually "moves." On top of that anchor, the EM reads a small portfolio of signals rather than lean on any one.

Guardrail — coach firstPod Lead owns delivery; the EM owns the person's growth and the sub-domain's health. Framed this way it reads as support, not surveillance — and the EM never becomes a bottleneck.

Cross-pod orchestration — honestly open

Who owns the layer above the pods — dependencies between pods, the sequencing of problems, delivery health as a system? Today it sits with Staff + Director; an Orchestrator role (Sandra is one candidate) is one way to make it explicit. Named openly as a decision to make, not hand-waved.

Your concerns, answered

What we heard — and how this responds

Mapped straight from the RTIE survey and the drop-in. Useful when socializing the model with the wider Domain.

Knowledge
Sub-domain expertise loss
Sub-domains are the direct answer: a stable home per set of services, a named SME (Bruno Silva, Miguel Monteiro, José Rosa), regular syncs on what pods changed, plus AI summaries and code dojos to keep knowledge alive. The Proficiency Matrix makes it measurable.
Operations
On-call structure unresolved
On-call rides on the sub-domain, not the pod — so it can't evaporate when a pod dissolves. In Phase 0 rotas are untouched; consolidation is only ever considered later, and only when proficiency supports it.
Roles
QA / QE ambiguity
QE is a capability alongside the sub-domains: it lives outside pods and is lent into problems, owning test strategy and automation — a coach raising the bar across pods, not manual QA at the end.
Coordination
Dependencies between pods
Owned by the cross-pod layer — today Staff (Dias) + Director; an Orchestrator role (Sandra a candidate) is one option. Modes are fixed by size: InnerSource / Embed / Parallel Problem (Operating model). Named openly as a decision to make.
Transition
Abrupt change
Because sub-domains retain ownership throughout, teams and pods coexisting is a feature — no service is left without a home during the shift.
Ownership
Losing clear ownership
Split cleanly: sub-domains own services and on-call; pods own outcomes for a problem. Ownership of the codebase never sits with a temporary pod.
People
EM role & career path
EMs stay allocated to people, keeping stability and growth continuous — with a defined oversight portfolio for rotation (Roles & governance). Pod Lead is a mantle, not a ladder rung; growth is tied to impact. The IC growth path is preserved.
Onboarding
How new joiners ramp
The sub-domain is the ramp: a stable home with an SME, syncs and runbooks gives new joiners the context and people a heads-down pod can't.
Reality check
Idle time waiting on discovery
The ~20% sub-domain allocation and the sub-domain backlog (upgrades, tech debt, resilience) absorb the gaps while a problem is still being framed.
Reality check
AI is not a silver bullet
Agreed — AI augments sub-domain knowledge (summaries, ramp) but doesn't replace experts. Proficiency stays human-owned and tracked.
